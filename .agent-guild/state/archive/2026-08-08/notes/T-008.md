## Accuracy note: the guard moved under me mid-task

When I first read `tests/lint.bats` it was 63 lines and anchored on a line's first non-whitespace token. I measured it directly: a planted `true; [[ 1 -eq 2 ]]` passed the guard green, while a line-start canary was caught. So my first draft told the reader the guard was narrower than the rule and that a `[[` after a semicolon walked past it.

T-007's rework then landed while I was writing. The file grew to ~98 lines, widened to command position (`;`, `{`, `&&`, `||`, `then`, `do`, `else`) with `if`/`while` conditions deliberately excluded, and my text became false. It changed a second time a few minutes later, and at that point it was transiently flagging its own pattern line and `mutation-check.sh:168`'s `if [[ ... ]]`.

Rather than chase it, I rewrote both files to describe the guard by its contract — fails the suite on a bare compound in command position, leaves `if`/`while` conditions alone because the branch consumes the status — and moved the first-token story into `decisionLog` as history, where it is durably true regardless of what the pattern looks like now. That is also just the better editorial call: working memory should not carry another file's regex.

The concurrency is worth flagging to the orchestrator on its own terms. My first two measurements of T-007's artifact were both of versions that no longer exist.
