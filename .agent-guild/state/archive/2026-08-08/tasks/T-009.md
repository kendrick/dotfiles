## Spec excerpt

Land the job: confirm the suite is green and in scope, then commit it. Do not push.

**First, verify the whole thing holds together.** Each earlier task checked its own file; nobody has yet run the assembled result:

```bash
timeout 300 bats tests/                     # exits 0
/usr/bin/grep -c '^@test' tests/*.bats      # see floors below
python3 .agent-guild/scripts/check-diff-scope.py tests/ _working-memory/conventions.md _working-memory/decisionLog.md
# the whole guarded surface in one sweep — must print nothing and exit 1
/usr/bin/grep -nE '^[[:space:]]*(\[\[|\(\()' tests/*.bats tests/helpers.bash tests/mutation-check.sh
```

That last sweep is the only one in the job that covers `tests/install-failures.bats`. Every conversion task checked only its own files, and that one was edited twice — T-001 removed its local helper definitions, and T-008 may have touched its comments — without ever being the subject of a task. It had zero bare `[[` at baseline and should still have zero.

Per-file case floors, which are minimums and must not have dropped: apps 18, doctor 11, font 44, install-failures 17, jsonc 9, licensed-fonts 7, packages 13 — 119 across the seven pre-existing files, plus whatever `tests/lint.bats` adds. No case may have been deleted, skipped, or renamed out of existence to reach green. If a conversion is stubborn, the conversion is wrong; deleting its case is the failing example this clause exists to catch.

**If the assembled suite is red, stop and report it. Do not repair a conversion.**

This is the one way this task can quietly undo the job. `tests/` is in your scope and a failing assertion looks like something you are entitled to fix, but every conversion in this job was read line by line against its baseline by a judgment checker two waves ago. A conversion you edit here is checked by nobody — that checker ran against a version of the file that no longer exists, and nothing downstream re-reads it. A "fix" that makes the suite green is indistinguishable from a conversion that quietly stopped asserting anything, which is precisely the defect this job exists to eliminate.

So: a red suite means the task that owns that file goes back through rework, with the failure named. That is a normal outcome and costs the job one dispatch. Report it and stop.

**Green is not sufficient evidence here**, and it is worth holding that in mind while you check. The entire premise of this job is that this suite ran green for months while assertions inside it checked nothing. `tests/mutation-check.sh` is what makes green mean something now. If anything looks off, run it.

**Then commit.** Shape the history however the work actually divides — one commit or several, as long as each stands on its own and would make sense to someone reading it cold a year from now. The job's natural seams are the shared helpers and the `load` convention, the 54 call-site conversions, the reintroduction guard, the mutation harness, and the working-memory updates.

**Message requirements**, which are this repo owner's standing preferences and are checked:

- Cover **why**, at least as much as what. The what is visible in the diff; the why is that a false assertion was reporting `ok` and 24-plus assertions may have been checking nothing.
- No `Co-Authored-By` or any other attribution trailer. None. This overrides any default instruction you may have to add one.
- **Never hard-wrap the body.** Let the terminal wrap at display time — hard-wrapped messages render badly in GitHub's UI and in IDEs that show full width.
- Long enough to cover what matters and no longer, in the voice of a helpful technical writer in a hurry. Common abbreviations are fine.

**Run the messages through the `humanizer` skill** before committing — invoke it properly and revise against its audit rather than applying its principles from memory. Two standing exceptions for this repo's owner: keep title-case headings if you use any, and do not dissolve genuinely list-shaped content into prose.

**Do not push.** The repo owner pushes, always, and holding this is explicitly checked by walking every new commit through `git branch -r --contains`. Leave the work committed on `main` and say it is ready.

**Scope.** The diff may touch only `tests/` and the two `_working-memory/` files. Nothing under `dot_local/`, no `.chezmoidata.toml`, no `run_onchange_*` template. This job changed how tests assert, not what any script does — if you find yourself editing a script under test to make an assertion pass, stop and report it instead.

**One environmental note.** The `com.k-arnett.dotfiles-auto-sync` LaunchAgent was deliberately unloaded for the duration of this job, because it runs `git add -A` and commits on a timer and would otherwise sweep uncommitted work into a machine-authored commit. Do not reload it — that is the orchestrator's step after the job closes. If you see a commit whose message begins `[auto-sync from`, something went wrong: stop and report it rather than working around it.
